# Engineering decisions (candidate 0.1.0)

* **Toolchain:** libGDX 1.14.2, Java 17 bytecode, Gradle 8.14.3 wrapper, AGP 8.7.3, compile/target SDK 35, minSdk 26. (The package's "Gradle 9.8.1" was not used: AGP/Gradle compatibility could not be checked offline.)
* **CI:** `workflow_dispatch` + tag pushes only (owner asked not to spend hosted CI during development), not every push as the spec says.
* **World:** circumference = 2*pi*10; one-way platform tops (jump up through, land on top); collisions are in wrapped arc space so falling can land on lower revolutions (setback, not instant death).
* **Failure:** falling more than 12 units below the last footing returns to the last checkpoint instantly (crumbled tiles restored). Checkpoint flags every rest platform.
* **Movement:** run 5.6 u/s, jump apex 1.94 u (hold) with variable height, coyote 0.12 s, buffer 0.13 s, pad launch 21/24 u/s (apex 6.5/8.5 u). All in `tuning.json`.
* **Controls:** floating stick (left) + jump anywhere on the right half; left-handed swap. No second button (grab is automatic).
* **Content:** 4 zones (Meadow, Frost, Dusk, Night = grass/snow blocks with palette tints and sky blends), 9 element types + grab/pull/catch as player abilities, 6 pre-validated 520 m towers (~330 elements, ~6 min of perfect autopilot play, intended ~15-20 min for a person).
* **Player model:** the owner supplied the Quaternius Ultimate Platformer Pack; its blue "Character" is a skinned 3D model with 18 animations and replaced the earlier voxel-extruded Foozle robot (kept under `tools/legacy_robot` only as the app-icon source). glTF -> g3dj conversion is a build script (gdx-gltf is not on Maven Central). Rope/cable/ledge hangs reuse the Run/Jump_Idle animations (no dedicated climb clips exist); the model has 29 bones, so `numBones` is 32 (GLES2 uniform budget is the risk on very old GPUs).
* **Assets gaps filled originally:** audio (synth), font (5x7 bitmap), UI, VFX, title art, icon.
* **Permissions:** `VIBRATE` only (normal permission). No INTERNET (OTA not implemented).
* **Deferred / not in this candidate:** over-the-air updates; colorblind palette modes (HUD uses shapes+text, not color alone, but no palette option); grappling hook (named once in the owner's words, absent from the traversal grammar - cable implemented); Play-Store release signing.
* **Idle personality:** after ~3.5 s standing still the hero runs random idle beats (Wave, head-shake No, nod Yes, Duck stretch, Punch), turning square to the camera for the fourth-wall ones, sometimes with a comic speech bubble (`HeroRig`, cosmetic RNG only, never affects the simulation). Resets the moment he moves.
* **Decoys:** after the route is built, 51-87 decoy elements per tower (dead-end spurs forward-and-down or back-and-up, crumbling-tip traps, floating "stepping-stone" lures) are added. They are plain STATIC/CRUMBLE tiles stored after the route (`routeCount`, `anchor`), never carry checkpoints or progress, are rejected if they touch the route, clash with another spiral layer, or sit within reach of later route (no shortcuts), and the generator re-validates the surrounding links with the real solver. The solver now looks one link ahead so decoys can't strand it. Falling onto a dead end is recoverable: step off and fall back to the last checkpoint.
* **Gripping poses:** the pack has no hang/climb clips, so while on a ledge, rope or cable `HeroRig` re-aims both arms after the animation each frame (ledge: forward and up toward the edge; rope/cable: straight up) and snaps the fists to the sim's grip point. On a ledge he turns 84 degrees to face it; pull-up blends the arms back down. Test hooks: `-Dclimb.hang=true` (parks him on a ledge), `-Dclimb.hangYaw=N`, `-Dclimb.heroDebug=true`.

## Endless climb (owner direction, build 17+)
* **Owner decisions (supersede the v6 package where they differ):** the climb is **endless** (no summit, best height is the score, six fixed towers retired); checkpoints are saved so a killed app resumes at the last checkpoint; the Quaternius hazards are in and should be "creative" puzzle traps; **crabs and a spiked club are in** (the package said no combat; the owner overrode it): crabs only shove, never hurt, and the club is a time-limited floating pickup; coloured keys open matching coloured castles.
* **Slices:** the tower is generated in ~60 m slices. Slice k is a pure function of (seed, k, slice k-1) (`CourseGenerator.chunk`), built on a background thread (`Tower`) while you climb, so the world never ends and a save only needs the slice the last checkpoint is in (`SaveData` v3: seed, slice, slice JSON, checkpoint index; floats are written exactly so a resumed tower regenerates identically). Each slice starts on the previous slice's last rest platform and carries the last 8 route elements as planner context plus the whole previous slice as geometry-only "ghosts" for spiral-layer clearance. A dead end during generation is retried with different (deterministic) choices.
* **Difficulty:** base difficulty ramps over `rampHeight` (520 m: gap sizes, mover amplitude, narrow platforms); hazards appear from `hazardStartY` and ramp to full frequency over `hazardRampY` (more hazards, two-hazard gaps, fewer rests, shorter hazard periods, slightly smaller required timing window). After about 1.2 km it plateaus at the hardest setting the solver can still prove fair; variety continues. The four worlds repeat (Meadow, Frost, Dusk, Night, "Meadow Base 2"...).
* **Hazards live in `Course.hazards`, not in the route**, so the route stays a simple ascending chain. Each is anchored to a route element and is a pure function of time (saw, cannon ball, trap, slab), so the solver can sweep their phase. Planner extensions: wait phases for periodic hazards, a hop-over policy for things in the middle of a platform, a walk-off policy for heading down, and a **timing window** metric (fraction of the cycle a move works in) that hazard links must meet. Hazards never sit in the standing room of a platform except the one they own; a hit sends you to the last checkpoint (like a fall) with 0.7 s of grace so nothing can chain-kill.
* **Keys and castles:** a castle is a 9 m platform with a tower that is a solid wall (7.5 m, cannot be jumped) until you carry its colour; the key is consumed. Every castle gets a **key room**: a platform below the path (or reached by a spring-pad return) that the solver must reach, collect the key on, and return from, validated from many standing positions against the final geometry. If no key room can be proven, the castle is **removed** instead, so a locked door can never be unopenable. Respawns keep keys; the key room is anchored at or after the checkpoint before the castle, so you can never be stuck behind it.
* **Found and fixed on the way:** the ledge pull-up interpolated across the whole ring for platforms past the first revolution (a rendering oddity in the old towers, a lethal bug once hazards existed).

## Creatures, character choice, sound pack (builds 20+)
* **Crabs and bees are not combat** (owner clarification): no health, no damage, no death. They only bump you, like ping-pong; the only way anything "dies" is that a crab or bee that the club hits is knocked off and falls. Falling is still the only way to lose progress. A bee is a pure function of time like the other hazards: it flies in high from one side, buzzes around its spot, dives, and flies off again until its next visit (`Element.beeS/beeY`), so the solver proves a timing window exists. Crab and bee use the animated Quaternius models (Walk / Flying clips).
* **Characters:** Settings -> CHARACTER switches between the Quaternius bunny (skinned, clips) and the owner-supplied chibi hamster (static model, so all of its life is body language in `HeroRig.updateHam`: breathing, run bounce and sway, jump stretch, landing squash, hang wiggle, idle gags). Applies live.
* **Sounds:** the owner's retro pack is in `assets/audio/pack`; `assets/data/sfx.json` maps game sounds to files (random pick per play); entries can be edited or removed (falls back to the synthesized sound). The mapping was chosen from file names and lengths without listening, so expect to remap some.
* **Unconfirmed licenses:** the music, the sound pack and the hamster were supplied by the owner without license text. They are credited as owner-supplied; confirm before any public release.

## Animation set (builds 21+)
* Both characters implement the same states (`HeroRig.Anim`): IDLE (with idle gags), RUN, JUMP, BIG_JUMP (pad/spring bounce: somersault), FALL (flailing), LAND, HANG, CLIMB (rope, hand over hand with kicking legs), SHIMMY (cable), PULLUP, HIT (shoved by a crab or bee, or hurt by a hazard: thrown back, arms and legs flung). The state is derived from the simulation (`pick`), not from the clip name.
* **Bunny:** Quaternius clips (Idle, Walk, Run, Jump, Jump_Idle, Jump_Land, HitReact, Wave/No/Yes/Duck/Punch) for the body; hang/climb arms and legs are bone overrides in code (the pack has no hang or climb clips).
* **Hamster:** body, arms, feet and ears are separate rigid parts (no skeleton in the supplied model), each posed per state in `updateHam`.
* Test hook: `-Dclimb.animCycle=true -Dclimb.character=0|1` cycles every state for contact-sheet screenshots.

## Falling, bridges, audio watchdog, title hero picker (owner request)
- **Falling:** a fall no longer ends at a fixed depth. In the endless climb the player respawns only when below the lowest platform of the simulated window (`Sim.floorY`); until then he can land on any lower level and carry on. Finite/planner sims keep the old depth rule.
- **Wooden bridges:** `SEESAW` element (plank on a central pivot; tilt state per element in the Sim, tips toward the player at `seesawRate`, slows uphill running and slides you downhill, levels out when empty) and a floating `STATIC` bridge look (`Element.skin = 1`), optionally with a rope hanging near its middle. Both are generator modules (`SEESAW`, `BRIDGE`) validated by the same planner. Bridge geometry is built from planks (the pack's bridge models are arched, which would not match the flat collision). All numbers in `tuning.json`.
- **Audio:** `Audio.update` watchdog restarts music that silently stopped and rebuilds the player. The 4-5 s dropout seen on a phone could not be reproduced on desktop; the watchdog is a mitigation, not a confirmed fix.
- **Title screen:** hero (bunny/hamster) button next to the menu.
- Finite test towers moved to seeds 200+; the finite-tower solver run still fails ~1 in 30 seeds (arrival-momentum plans, bee timing) while 500+ endless slices solve cleanly.

## Ramps (owner request: "use the ramp however you can")
- `RAMP` element (Space Kit model, slope 0.576, length 2.9-3.9 m): a walkway up to a block taller than a jump. The surface height is evaluated at the player's own position along it (same mechanism as the seesaw).
- Variants from world 2: **crumbling** (falls apart 0.5 s after you step on it; Build 72), **shaky** (Build 72: throws you up and sideways every 0.25 s with a trampoline flip), **spinning** (Build 72, replaced sinking: turns like a record), plus a **ski jump** layout (leap off the top across a gap). All validated by the same planner; none can hurt, they only cost position.
- Seeds for the finite test towers moved to 300+; the whole-tower solver run still fails for roughly 1 in 30 finite seeds and about 1 in 170 endless slices (plan from an arrival state; unrelated to ramps).

## Characters rebuilt on one skeleton (owner: hamster animation was bad)
- The hamster is now a **real skinned model** (`tools/hamster/build_rigged_hamster.py`): the Quaternius character skeleton (same bone names and rest rotations as the bunny) with every joint moved to a hamster landmark, skin weights from position (spine gradient, arm stubs, legs/feet, ears, rigid head), and **all of the bunny's clips** with translation keys re-based on the hamster's bone lengths. It therefore idles, walks, runs, jumps, lands, takes hits, waves and nods exactly like the bunny. While gripping a rope/ledge the stubby arms stretch (cartoon reach) and angle inward so the paws meet the rope; the hamster faces the camera on ropes like the bunny does.
- Four **Space Kit astronauts** (Finn the Frog, Rae the Red Panda, Fernando the Flamingo, Barbara the Bee) are selectable. They ship rigged with 18 clips; finger bones are dropped (shader limit 32 bones) and the pistol is hidden. `render/Characters.java` lists all six; Settings and the title button cycle through them.
- Juice pass for every character: lean into the run / tuck when rising / reach when falling, per-character voice pitch for grunts, hits and effort sounds.

## Space biome (owner request: "Add space as a biome")
- A **fifth world, Deep Space**, follows Night Summit; the five repeat every 650 m (`zoneHeight` 130 m x `zoneCount` 5 in `tuning.json`; base difficulty still ramps over `rampHeight`). Generator `theme()` and the renderer, HUD bar and banner all use the same count.
- Look: near-black sky with two star layers, no clouds or weather, space dust motes, the Space Kit planets larger and nearer with a second planet and a third rock per band inside the spiral, twice as many ship fly-bys. Platforms are a metal deck with beacon lights hanging from a Space Kit asteroid (crumbling platforms are rust-coloured with orange lights). The stars and planets fade back to the meadow sky over the last quarter of the world.
- Gameplay is unchanged in this biome (same solver-validated modules); a low-gravity rule was not added because it would need new planner rules.

## Red gem checkpoints, long biomes, more castles (owner request)
- **Checkpoints are red gems** that hover above rest platforms (bob, wobble, pulse, drop sparks, soft glow shell), placed by height: about every 30 m (`gemSpacing`, +-15% random give), never right before a slice's own closing gem; a dropped castle attempt can no longer leave two gems close together. Reaching one pops it (burst + sound + toast); passed gems stay as small dim markers. Respawning behind a reached gem resets nothing.
- **Biomes are 400 m each** (`zoneHeight`), so one lap of the five worlds is 2,000 m; base difficulty still ramps over 520 m and hazards over their own ramp. Colour blends over the last 20% of each world.
- **Coloured castles with hidden matching keys** (already built) now appear about every 110-120 m instead of every ~270 m (every slice may host one; rest platforms of 4 m+ qualify). Each castle still gets a provable key room or is removed. The solver was taught to line itself up and shuffle along the platform before the key detour, which fixed key-room failures that came from knife-edge start positions.

## Speed-run clock (owner request)
- The top-left HUD shows **height**, **best**, then **TOWER <metres climbed since the last castle opened> <time since the last castle opened>** (the tower being worked towards) and **TOTAL <time of the whole climb>**, always visible.
- The clock counts only while playing (not paused, not in menus, not during hit-stop), starts on the first input of a climb, keeps running through falls and respawns, and survives quitting (it is part of the save). NEW CLIMB resets it.
- Opening a castle records a **split** (time since the previous opening or the start) and the **total at that moment** (the last one is the total time to the last tower unlock), shown in a toast with "FASTEST TOWER!" / "PACE PB!" when beaten. Personal bests (fastest single tower, fastest clock at the Nth opening) persist across climbs. The pause menu lists the splits.
- Climbs use a fresh random seed, so splits compare as pace, not as identical-route times; a fixed-seed "race" mode for runners is possible later (the generator is deterministic per seed).

## Rope/cable grip and ledge dangle rework (owner feedback on build 33)
- Rope and cable climbing now put **both hands on the line**: each arm is aimed from its shoulder at a point on the rope (one hand high, one low, swapping hand over hand while climbing; on a plain hang one hand sits a little higher), and the hero is drawn a little behind the line so his arms visibly reach forward and grip it. Legs draw their knees up alternately while climbing and dangle with a slow sway on a hang. (A bug where the aim target was overwritten by the bone's current direction had left the arms spread sideways.)
- The ledge-hang leg flail and body tremble were far too violent (and looked broken on the long-legged astronauts): amplitude and speed cut to roughly a third.

## Step-up onto close platforms (owner bug report on build 34)
- Walking or dropping off a platform towards a close platform that is a little higher no longer slips through its edge. The ledge grab used to need the hands to be level with or below the top, which only happens after a jump; when the feet are already within a hand's height below the top the player now scrambles straight up onto it (a "step-up", the pull-up animation started from where the feet are). A hand-height or more below the top still gives the normal hanging grab. `StepUpTest` covers gaps of 0.2-1.0 m and heights of 0 to 0.9 m, and fails without the change. The solver stress run is unchanged (168 sections, 0 failures).

## Ten equal castles and a timed finish (owner, 2026-10-09)
- The castles of the endless climb stand at EXACT, equal heights: tuning `castleSpacing` = 500, so castle N's deck (and gate) is at N x 500 m, for every runner and every seed; what lies between them (obstacles) still varies by seed. The old frequent castles are replaced by these. Generator: the slice that starts within 80 m below a castle height builds it (CourseGenerator.buildExactCastle: a rest platform, a staircase of small steps that lands the deck at exactly the target, the deck with its gate; `gate.skin` = castle number). Modules in the approach window may not carry the climb past the target (tryModuleBelow). If a side key room cannot be proven for an exact castle, its key lies on the rest platform in front of the castle (roomFallback) - a castle is never dropped.
- Worlds are 1000 m each (zoneHeight 1000): castles 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 stand on world changes (meadow, frost, dusk, night, deep space); the progress bar spans 5000 m.
- Finish: tuning `finishCastle` = 10. Opening its gate with the key and walking to the castle's centre ends the timed run (Sim.finishedRun, EV_FINISH): the run clock freezes at that moment, the time is recorded (SaveData.finished / finishTime / bestFinish), and a screen offers END RUN AND SAVE TIME (fresh climb next time, best time kept) or KEEP CLIMBING FOR EVER (free-play, clock stays frozen, HUD shows FINISH time). Per-castle splits keep working.

## Official 5,000 m run: spec confirmation and gem checkpoints (owner, 2026-10-09)

## Solver investigation before Build 42 (seed 32 slice 4, seed 130 slice 11, and two more found by the sweep)
All four were reproduced on the real generated geometry and traced with a live-sim harness. None was a physically impossible transition; three were solver false negatives and one exposed a real hole in the generator's proof:
- **Seed 32 slice 4 (key detour):** the solver arrived on the anchor platform with momentum and then stood idle, sliding off the far edge before planning the detour. The solver now brakes and centres like a person (`Autopilot.settle`).
- **Seed 130 slice 11, seed 194 slice 0:** a decoy ledge hangs just above the spring / the next platform. A full-speed run-up from the far end of the platform overshoots onto that ledge; a nearer or gentler approach lands cleanly (confirmed from the centred state: plan succeeds). - **Seed 178 slice 0 (genuine generator-proof gap):** the key branch landing at platform 53 sits directly under route platform 24. The pair proof (`Sim.setWindowNear`) now also simulates earlier route platforms that hang within 12 m / 9 m of the endpoints. - **Regression found on the way:** after hazards became knockbacks (no respawn) the solver's and generator's `falls` check no longer rejected moves that touch a hazard. They now use `Sim.setbacks()` (falls + hits), so a proven move is again a clean move.
- Knockback is now 6 m/s sideways and 6 m/s up (it was 4 and 3), so a hit can actually push you off a narrow platform.

Evidence: seeds 100-259 (2,040 slices) and 1-99 (6 slices each) swept with the autopilot: 0 failed runs, 0 generation errors (before the fixes: 5 failures in 1,200 slices, 10 with knockback and no hit check). `SolverEvidenceTest` re-runs the four original slices and re-proves every key-branch link; `FallRulesTest` verifies knockback, landing on what is below, and the red-gem return after leaving the map.

## Resume softlock and clock drift (found by the read-only Eternal Run audit)
- **Softlock (HIGH, fixed):** a resumed climb has only the stored slice (the one holding the last gem), and keys carried / castles opened were not saved. Each castle's key lies at a random height in the 500 m section before it, so after an app kill (or MAIN MENU -> CONTINUE) at the 250 m gem the key could be in a slice that no longer exists, in front of a gate that is a wall without it (AUTOMATED: 6 of 8 seeds, castle 1, key 99-206 m below the gem). Fix (`ResumeState`): keys carried and the highest opened castle are saved (`SaveData.keysHeld`, `openedUpTo`; additive, version 5 unchanged) and restored on resume; opened castles stay open; a castle counts and is timed once (no duplicate split or false FASTEST TOWER after walking its door again); and if the next castle's key lay below everything that exists after the resume, it is handed over (never when it is still ahead). Tests: `ResumeStateTest`.
- **Run clock drift (fixed):** the speed-run clock was a float adding 1/60 s per step; its step size changes as it grows (+2.4 s at 30 min, -2.8 s at 1 h, -17 s at 2 h, -31 s at 3 h). It now counts whole steps (`SaveData.runTicks`) and derives the seconds; exact to 3 h in the test. Clocks saved by older builds carry on from their value.
- **Left as is (reported, low):** `Sim.time` (mover and hazard phase) is a float restarting each session: movers run 0.4% slow from 0.57 h of one session and drift further after ~4.5 h; `Tower.slices` retains a second copy of every slice (~69 KB/km, ~7 MB at 104 km); `Sim.setRange` rescans the tower for its lowest platform once per new slice (~1.5 ms at 104 km); `SaveData.legacy` grows by ~150 B per archived run; falls past the resumed slice's lowest platform (below it after a restart) respawn at the checkpoint instead of landing on now-missing platforms (24-28% of drops from inside the resumed slice).

## Eternal Run architecture (build 43 candidate): bounded memory, floating origin, new save format
Owner directive: memory must not grow with height, extreme falls must land on real earlier platforms, coordinates must stay precise at any height, and old climb saves may be retired once.
- **Slices are the unit of storage.** Slice k = f(seed, k, the whole previous slice), so a slice cannot be rebuilt from a small carry; every slice is therefore stored losslessly compressed (`SliceCodec`, ~1.05 KB each, ~16 KB per km, bit-exact round trip tested). Only the slices within 300 m of the player are expanded into the shared `Tower.world` (`REACH`, 120 m hysteresis `MARGIN`); everything else is bytes. History is append-only on disk (`HistoryStore`, `history.bin`; a torn tail is ignored).
- **Framed generation.** Each slice is generated in its own small local frame (`yBase`/`sBase`; generator maths stays absolute through `CourseGenerator.base`), so no float ever holds a large coordinate. The world is expressed relative to a floating origin (`originY`, a multiple of 500 m; `originS`, whole laps). A rebuild produces a `Remap` (old->new element/hazard indices, dy) and `Sim.rebase` transplants the player's state; physics is unchanged by a rebuild (27 rebuilds in `TowerStreamingTest`, worst divergence 1.7e-4 m). Renderer state (camera, particles, clouds, space bands, zone/background) is shifted or computed from absolute height.
- **Identity.** Platforms are identified by (slice, local index); a shared start platform is the previous slice's last route element. Collected keys and opened gates are re-applied after a slice is reloaded (`doneFeat`, `openedUpTo`).
- **Falls.** The fall rule is unchanged (only passing below the lowest platform of the whole tower returns the player to the last red gem). `Sim.floorOverride` carries that height; the respawn is deferred until the checkpoint's slice is loaded (`respawnPending`). Verified against a whole-tower oracle: 40 drops landed on the same platform with the same arc position.
- **Generator dead end.** Rarely (~1 slice in 500 in a framed chain) a previous slice leaves no valid continuation; after 12 normal attempts the generator ignores the previous slice's layer-clearance ghosts (`relaxed`) rather than failing.
- **Save format (owner decision: compatibility relaxed).** `SaveData` v6 stores seed, checkpoint (slice, local), keys held, castles opened, run ticks and the climb's version stamp. A climb saved by an older format is archived under LEGACY RUNS, the player sees a one-time notice and starts a fresh climb; settings and personal bests are kept; nothing is deleted. A failure to rebuild a stored climb takes the same path instead of crashing.

## Platform edges and a generator hole found by them (version 1.1.5)
- **Owner report:** a jump into the waist of a moving platform slid through it and fell through. Root cause (reproduced in the simulator): platform sides are not solid and the grab / step-up rules switch off while the body rises faster than 3.5 m/s, so a fast rising jump slips inside the block; at the top of the jump the feet are below the top and the head above it, nothing lands or grabs it, and it sinks through.
- **Fix:** `Sim.trackSideEntry` / `catchInside`: a body that slips into a platform's footprint from the side while its feet are below the top is remembered and caught the moment it slows below 3.5 m/s (scramble up, hang range included, bounce on a pad or spring). No flight changes unless it would have ended in sinking through the block, so existing routes are untouched. A first attempt (a solid wall at the side face) was measured and rejected: it blocked flights that pass through decoy platforms and broke 6 existing proofs.
- **Root-cause test:** `EdgeContactTest` sweeps every platform kind the game uses (static x4 skins, crumble, movers H/V/Z x skins, swing, pad, spring, ramps x4 skins, seesaw; narrowest, widest, fastest), both sides, 7 depths, 7 vertical and 4 horizontal speeds, 2 gaps, 2 phases: about 185,000 trials, 30,000 side contacts. Before the fix 6.4-7% of side contacts fell through on EVERY kind (depth sliders 42% by the same oracle); now 0. Known limit: a very fast, rising, horizontal jump into a NARROW platform can still pass straight through it (it is not caught because it never sinks).
- **Generator hole found by the new layouts:** a slice whose red-gem rest came out narrower than 3.5 m marked the previous slice's platform (a context copy) as the gem, which is lost, leaving a whole 500 m section without a gem (a fall there would cost 500 m); the baseline already had gems up to 6.4 m from the midpoint. Fixed: the gem is chosen from this slice's own platforms, slightly before the midpoint (`GEM_LEAD` 4 m), with a rest rebuilt up to three times until it is wide enough; a slice that climbs past an exact-height structure is rejected and rebuilt. Measured over 12 seeds x 10 gems: worst deviation 3.43 m (baseline 6.41 m).
- **Gates:** full suite 130 tests, 0 failures; solver sweeps 480 absolute and 400 stored-slice (framed) slices, 0 failures, 0 generation errors. The tower generated for a given seed differs from build 43 (stored climbs are unaffected: slices are saved as bytes).

## Character hands, rope grip and hamster arms (controlled repair, 2026-10-13)
Diagnosis and plan: `docs/HAND_ROPE_FORENSIC_AUDIT.md` (Addenda A-B) and `docs/CHARACTER_REPAIR_PLAN.md`; validated with the faithful desktop harness `desktop/src/test/.../GameplayShot.java` (real `WorldRenderer`/`HeroRig` on real simulated frames, phone aspect), which reproduced the Build 55 device screenshot.

## Build 64: course codes, seeded runs, per-course times, HUD switches, taglines - 2026-10-14

Build 63's releases stay as they are; no build is dispatched until the owner authorises it. Each item records its design and why.
- **Course code (`SeedCode`).** A seed is a 64-bit number, so the code is 13 symbols of a 31-symbol alphabet (`23456789ABCDEFGHJKMNPQRSTUVWXYZ`: no 0 / O, 1 / I / L, so a code read off a screenshot or over the phone cannot be mistyped), because 31^13 is the smallest such power above 2^64; every 64-bit value has exactly one code and every 13-symbol string at or below the code of the all-ones seed is a seed (unsigned arithmetic, big-endian, leading `2`s are zeros). Shown and entered grouped `XXXXX-XXXX-XXXX`; entry is case-insensitive and ignores spaces and dashes; a bad entry says why ("A COURSE CODE NEVER HAS '0'", "... HAS 13 CHARACTERS (9 SO FAR)", a value past the last code "IS NOT A COURSE CODE"). The raw number is never shown or typed anywhere. A random run still draws its seed as before (31 bits, odd), so a random course's code starts with a run of `2`s; a typed code may name any seed, and the generator takes all of them the same way (`DeterminismTest`: a typed code and the random draw it came from grow the same tower).
- **Rules version (`CourseGenerator.RULES_VERSION` = 1, `rulesOfBuild`).** The generator has been deterministic per seed since the chunked design (every slice is a pure function of seed, slice index and the previous slice; enemies, cannons and crabs included; the proof runs under the same rules), but a time only means something against the same course, and Build 63 changed what a seed produces (cannon reach, the crab chase changed the generator's proofs). Determinism is proved to 5,600 m (past castle 10, into the endless climb), from a history resume, and for different seeds giving different towers.

SHOW BEST TIMES (default ON) hides the PB marks and the "vs best" comparisons from the gameplay HUD, the pause panel and the finish summary; it never touches the clock, the recording of times, or the times table. ## Build 72 root cause: streaming vs whole-tower divergence (owner deep-research directive)

- **Experiment** (`StreamingReplayTest`): one autopilot climb on seed 12 (26,000 steps, 411 m). Its exact inputs were replayed with no controller, (a) into the same tower and (b) into a streaming tower rebuilt around a different floating origin every 400 steps (60 rebuilds).
- **Result: physics, not the controller (Case A).**
  - (a) The same frame is identical bit for bit.
  - (b) The first difference is one float ulp (about 1.5e-5 m) at step 400, the first origin shift. Before anything else differs, the gap grows to at most 1.1 mm.
  - At step 24030 a landing happens one step apart (a 0.37 mm height difference at the moment of touchdown). 7 steps differ in mode or surface in all, and the open-loop gap peaks at 0.46 m. After that the gap closes again.
  - The climb is the same climb: the same 341 surfaces are stood on in the same order, and it ends 0.2 mm apart.
- **First causal divergence:** heights and arcs (`Sim.y`, `Sim.s`, every `Element.s/y`) are floats relative to the tower's origin (`Tower.originY`, `originS`). A rebuild re-expresses them around the new origin, and every later sum then rounds at a different magnitude. A rebuild that keeps `originY` still differs, at step 11601, because `originS` follows the window's lowest slice. Nothing is stale, misnumbered or skipped: element identities, crumble state and hazards remap exactly.
- **Why the controller looked guilty:** the autopilot is a bang-bang controller. A millimetre of rounding eventually moves one decision by one step, so two independently controlled runs part there. That is an amplified symptom, not a second cause.
- **Research** (`reports/Deterministic simulation divergence.md`, not in the repository):
  - Java 17 float arithmetic is strict IEEE everywhere, so the same operations in the same frame reproduce. The game confirms this.
  - Floating-origin rebasing preserves precision, not bit equivalence. The only exact cures are one absolute frame for the simulation, or fixed-point state. Doubles shrink the drift to about 1e-10 m but do not remove it.
- **Decision: no physics change in Build 72.** Every exact cure changes the simulation's arithmetic, and so the generator's proofs and possibly seeded courses. That needs the owner's approval (the directive's list) and is offered as a follow-up.
  - Nothing the player sees depends on two frames agreeing. A climb runs in one deterministic frame schedule. Run snapshots store absolute positions and identities, not inputs. Speedrun times come from the one live simulation.
  - The proofs' routes have margins far above a millimetre (no frame-perfect inputs).
- **Tests, reviewed:**
  - **`TowerStreamingTest`'s flip detection:** it compares decisions before the step for any difference. This is kept, and now justified by the replay: a flip can only come from that rounding.
  - **`RunSnapshot`'s `absS0`:** kept. It is state the step reads (where the step began), so a snapshot without it cannot resume exactly. It is optional, so older files load.
  - **`StreamingReplayTest`** asserts:
    - an exact same-frame replay;
    - no difference before the first origin shift;
    - under 2 mm until a timing difference;
    - at most 20 steps of event differences;
    - an identical surface sequence;
    - a final gap under 0.1 m.
- **humanHold (6 steps = 100 ms, autopilot only):** kept. Measured human key holds are about 72-202 ms (most 85-126 ms), and Mario AI holds actions 2 ticks (about 80 ms). Without a hold, a solver with a direction-only stick can dither every frame and get a half-speed walk no person can produce, and prove routes on it. It never touches the player's own simulation.
- **Open question for the owner (not changed):** the simulation calls `Math.sin`, `Math.cos` and `Math.atan2` (35 sites in `Sim`, `Element`, `CourseGenerator`, `CheckpointGem`: movers, swings, the cylinder). Java only promises these to within about 1 ulp, and Android's runtime uses its own native routines. So a phone may build a slightly different course from the same seed code than the desktop does, if a proof sits on a knife edge. Switching them to `StrictMath` (identical everywhere) leaves the desktop generation fingerprint unchanged (tried: `DeterminismTest` passes, `58ece4dd45fb787dfae1f63d`). On a phone it could change a course, which is a seeded-generation change, so it waits for approval.
- **Owner decision on StrictMath: deferred.** Physics changes only once a change has been shown to resolve the origin-shift divergence. Note that `StrictMath` would not do that by itself. It addresses a different risk: the phone and the desktop may disagree. The origin-shift drift is plain float re-rounding, so a targeted follow-up would need to:
  - simulate in one absolute frame, or use fixed-point;
  - prove it with `StreamingReplayTest`, which would then have to show a streaming replay that is bit-exact.

## Build 74: cross-platform determinism and competitive timing (owner directive, 2026-10-22)

Requirement: the same seed, the same inputs on the same simulation steps, give the same run, the same finish step and the same recorded time on Windows, Linux and Android.

**Causes found (with evidence) and fixed:**
1. **Respawns waited for the next rendered frame** (`PlayScreen` maintained the tower once per frame). Any simulation steps left in that frame were frozen, yet counted by the run clock and swallowing their inputs.
   - A fall therefore cost 0 to 5 extra steps depending on frame rate. `PacingProbe`: 174 frozen steps and a different climb under uneven pacing, from the same inputs.
   - Fix: `PlayScreen.keepTower` runs before every simulation step. A steady 60 fps device behaves exactly as before.
2. **Origin shifts shifted stored platform heights** (`Sim.rebase` subtracted the shift from `ey0`/`ey1`). A platform rebuilt around the new origin can differ from the shifted copy in the last bit.
   - Static platforms keep that height for good, and whether a far platform was resident during a shift depended on how far ahead the tower had been generated.
   - `ReplayTool diff`: same inputs with the tower generated 150 m vs 400 m ahead gave a 1-ulp landing difference at step 56,683, then a different climb (5 doors instead of 9, no finish).
   - Fix: the current height is taken from the rebuilt element (`e.yAt(time)`). Movers, seesaws and ramps recompute it every step anyway.
3. **Window decisions depended on the background generator's progress.** Generated slices joined the window as soon as the thread finished them, so rebuilds (and origin shifts) happened at device-dependent steps. With the fix above, the next difference was a rebuild at step 57,258 in one run only.
   - Fix: `Tower.admitAbs`. Slices join the window only once their bottom is at or below the highest point climbed + 100 m (`PlayScreen.ADMIT_AHEAD`), checked every step. If the thread has not finished a needed slice, it is made on the spot (never skipped, never partial). The thread still works 150 m ahead, so that wait is normally never needed.
4. **Transcendental maths:** `Math.sin`/`cos`/`atan2` may differ by an ulp between JVMs/ART. All 35 simulation and generation calls now use `StrictMath` (fdlibm, identical everywhere).
   - Measured before the switch: replays and generation were unchanged with every result nudged ±1 ulp (results are stored as floats). The switch removes the residual risk.
   - Desktop course fingerprints are unchanged.

**Timing (owner decision):**
- The clock is the integer step count at 60 steps/s (`SaveData.runTicks`), independent of frame rate, device speed and wall time. Resolution is 1/60 s ≈ 16.667 ms. No interpolated sub-step finish.
- **Rankings and ties:** compared in whole steps (`RunRecord.ticksOf`/`faster`: finish, legs, totals, per-seed bests, splits, the Express Elevator achievement). Every stored time is a step count / 60, recovered exactly below 18 hours.
- **Display:** the HUD/times text is derived from the step count (6 steps per tenth). The game shows **m:ss.t** (tenths, truncated), not MM:SS.mmm. Unchanged pending the owner's decision.

**Not changed:**
- simulation rate, movement, physics, course generation (fingerprints identical), saves and records (same fields; comparisons only);
- the hero's own position is still re-expressed at an origin shift, but the shift happens at the same step on every device;
- input is sampled per rendered frame, so a 30 fps device can change input only every second step. That is fairness, not determinism; it is documented and not changed.

**Verification:**
- `ReplayTool` (record/replay/diff) plays climbs exactly as `PlayScreen` does. Recorded: seed 12 to the castle-10 finish (472,941 steps, 9 castle doors; 35 harness SKIP actions where the autopilot stalls) and seed 21 with a respawn every 20,000 steps.
- The seed 12 replay is identical with generation 150 m, 400 m and 1,000 m ahead.
- `determinism.yml` replays the files on Linux, Windows (desktop JVMs) and Android (ART, x86_64 emulator) and requires byte-identical output.
- A real ARM phone is not covered (owner did not choose the in-app check).
